Surgical platforms

Optical fingerprinting across surgical platforms

Our imaging hardware and software work together to map tissue fluorescence. The same method can be adapted to the instrument a surgeon uses to see the operative field.

Surgical configurations

Three views of the operative field

Exoscope

The OncoLight imaging head integrates with an articulated robotic arm to capture high-resolution color and hyperspectral images simultaneously in real time as part of the surgical workflow.

Microscope

Our hyperspectral module couples to the optical path of existing commercial surgical microscopes. It is designed to capture spectral images in real time while the surgeon continues to work through the microscope.

Endoscope

We’re developing a real-time hyperspectral imaging platform for endoscopic and laparoscopic procedures.

Image processing

From the imaging head to the surgical display

The camera records light at each point in the field. Our software estimates how much of that signal comes from the fluorescent agent and aligns the resulting map with the color view.

Explore the five processing stages
  1. Illuminate. The imaging head delivers light to excite fluorescence in the tissue.
  2. Capture. A snapshot camera records a spectrum at each pixel across the 400–900 nm band. A second camera records the anatomy in color.
  3. Calibrate. Reference measurements account for the instrument’s response at each wavelength.
  4. Separate and correct. The software separates overlapping emissions and accounts for tissue absorption and scattering.
  5. Display. The resulting map can be viewed alongside the color image or as an overlay.
Optical agents under study

Our first focus is protoporphyrin IX (PpIX), the fluorescent molecule that can accumulate in glioma after 5-ALA administration. We are also studying fluorescein and indocyanine green (ICG). Each has a distinct emission spectrum that the software can use to estimate its contribution.
